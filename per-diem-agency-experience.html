Working Per Diem Through an Agency: My Experience (From a Bay Area Nurse)

By Casey, RN

This is my own experience with one agency. Others may work differently.

The short version: for me, it pays less than my hospital job, but I get variety, autonomy, flexibility and tax perks.

As a Bay Area nurse, I've found it hard to find a side hustle or per diem agency gig that pays close to my hospital hourly wage. But I find that taking a pay cut helps me over the long run. Here's how and why I do it on the side.

How it works for me

Finding a shift

I work with an agency that has an app. When I want a shift, I turn on notifications and it pings my phone with new jobs. I can also browse to see what's open. I set my search radius wide (several hundred miles), and I'll drive a couple of hours if the price is right.

Getting the job

When I see a job, I can see the type of treatment, how many hours it will take, and how much I'll get paid per hour. I click on it and share my hours of availability. In my experience, the patient often has a preference, so if I can work with it, I'm more likely to get the job. Once I submit my interest, I wait for a confirmation message.

The day before

I call the patient to confirm. I ask about things like pets and parking, and whether their medications have already been delivered.

The day of

I pack my own equipment: BP cuff, SpO2 monitor, thermometer, laptop, hand sanitizer, mask, and shoe covers (I always bring them to be polite). The patient already has their medications and supplies for treatment. I arrive, introduce myself, and get straight to work. Sometimes I'm there for 30 minutes for an injection, and sometimes it's a full day of infusions.

Training and support

My agency wants things to go well, so they made sure I had training first. They gave me reading materials on the equipment and medications, and I shadowed another nurse for a visit (unpaid). They also gave me great resources, including who to call with questions.

What I like about it

Something new

I like to mix it up. I could pick up extra shifts at my hospital job, but sometimes I want a break from that. It's nice to simply have another option.

Keeping up my skills

I work with a home immunotherapy infusion agency, which is a different population from my women's health niche. I start IVs every day with my patients, and it's something I enjoy honing. I work with all genders, young and old, with different diseases and comorbidities.

No managers

I enjoy the autonomy. No one is looking over my shoulder. That also means I need to be comfortable managing patients myself and knowing when to escalate to EMS.

Tax perks

I work with this agency as an independent contractor, which means I'm self-employed and handle my own taxes, including self-employment tax. Some business expenses can be a write-off, like a home office if you have a space you use regularly and only for work. Check with a tax professional about your own situation.

Flexibility

Probably the best thing for me is that I only work when I want to. There isn't a required number of shifts per month like I've seen in the hospital.

The downsides, for me

The pay is often less than my hospital job

It's the tradeoff I accept for the flexibility.

The pay can change

In my experience, the more nurses available in an area, the less the pay. But if they need a shift filled fast, I've seen a big spike. Other nurses are looking at the same thing, so if I see something I want, I jump on it.

There's unpaid time

Driving to the job, calling to confirm and shadowing for training all happen on my own time. My agency also requires CEUs, certifications and malpractice insurance, which I complete on my own time and dime.

They can change the model at any time

I didn't sign a contract promising me a certain number of shifts or a certain amount of pay, so they could change that whenever they want. They could also change the requirements, like adding charting that takes much longer than a shift allows. That's why I use this only as supplementary income, never as my main source.

If you're thinking about it

If you already have a main job and want variety and flexibility, it might be worth a look. Just go in knowing the pay can be lower and nothing is guaranteed.
